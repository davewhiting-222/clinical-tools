Understanding Polymyalgia Rheumatica (PMR)

Prepared by your family doctor

April 23, 2026

What is Polymyalgia Rheumatica?

Polymyalgia rheumatica (PMR) is an inflammatory condition that causes muscle pain and stiffness, particularly in the shoulders, neck, and hips. The name literally means "many muscle pains" in Greek. PMR primarily affects people over 50, with the average age being around 70 years old.

Key Point: PMR is not just "normal aging." It's a real medical condition that responds well to treatment. You don't have to live with the pain and stiffness.

What Causes PMR?

The exact cause of PMR isn't fully understood, but researchers believe it involves:

  • Immune system activation: Your body's immune system mistakenly attacks healthy tissue, causing inflammation
  • Genetic factors: Certain genes may make you more susceptible
  • Environmental triggers: Infections or stress may trigger the condition in genetically predisposed individuals
  • Age-related changes: Changes in the immune system that occur with aging

How PMR Shows Up

PMR symptoms typically develop over weeks to months and can vary from person to person. The most common signs include:

Primary Symptoms

  • Morning stiffness: Lasting more than 45 minutes, often several hours
  • Muscle pain and aching: Especially in shoulders, neck, and hips
  • Difficulty with daily activities: Getting out of bed, raising arms, climbing stairs
  • Fatigue: Feeling unusually tired and weak

Other Common Symptoms

  • Low-grade fever
  • Loss of appetite
  • Unintentional weight loss
  • Depression or mood changes
  • General feeling of being unwell
Important: If you experience sudden vision changes, jaw pain while chewing, or severe headaches along with PMR symptoms, contact your doctor immediately. These could be signs of giant cell arteritis, a related but more serious condition.

How We Treat PMR

The Good News About Treatment

PMR responds remarkably well to treatment with corticosteroids (prednisone). Most people experience dramatic improvement within days to weeks of starting treatment.

Treatment Success: The majority of people with PMR can expect to return to their normal activities and quality of life with proper treatment.

Our Treatment Plan

Initial Treatment: I typically start patients on prednisone 20 mg daily for the first 3 months. This dose is usually very effective at controlling symptoms and reducing inflammation.

Gradual Reduction: After 3 months, we slowly reduce the prednisone dose over many months. This gradual tapering helps prevent symptoms from returning while allowing your body to adjust.

Prednisone Weaning Schedule (Typical)

Time Period Typical Dose What to Expect
Months 1-3 20 mg daily Dramatic symptom improvement
Months 4-6 15 mg daily Continue feeling well
Months 7-9 10 mg daily Monitor for any return of symptoms
Months 10-12 7.5 mg daily Slower reductions from here
Year 2+ 5 mg and below Very gradual tapering to zero

Understanding Prednisone Side Effects

While prednisone is very effective for PMR, it can cause side effects, especially with long-term use. Understanding these helps us work together to minimize risks.

Common Side Effects

  • Increased appetite and weight gain: Focus on healthy eating and portion control
  • Mood changes: May feel more energetic, anxious, or emotional
  • Sleep changes: Difficulty falling asleep or staying asleep
  • Increased thirst and urination: Monitor blood sugar levels
  • Easy bruising: Skin becomes more fragile

More Serious Long-term Concerns

  • Bone loss (osteoporosis): We'll monitor bone density and may recommend calcium, vitamin D, and sometimes bone-strengthening medications
  • Diabetes: Regular blood sugar monitoring, especially if you have risk factors
  • High blood pressure: Regular monitoring and treatment if needed
  • Cataracts and glaucoma: Regular eye exams
  • Increased infection risk: Be cautious around sick people, stay up-to-date with vaccines
Remember: We use the lowest effective dose for the shortest time possible. The benefits of treating PMR usually far outweigh the risks of side effects, especially when we monitor carefully.

When Prednisone Weaning Becomes Difficult

Some people have trouble reducing their prednisone dose without symptoms returning. This doesn't mean treatment has failed—it just means we need a different approach.

Signs You May Need Additional Help

  • Symptoms return every time we try to reduce prednisone below a certain dose
  • You've been on prednisone for more than 2 years
  • You're experiencing significant side effects from prednisone
  • Blood markers of inflammation remain elevated

When I Refer to Rheumatology

If we're having difficulty weaning you off prednisone, I'll refer you to a rheumatologist (arthritis specialist). They may recommend adding methotrexate, a medication that can help reduce inflammation and allow us to use lower doses of prednisone.

Methotrexate Benefits: This "steroid-sparing" medication can help control PMR inflammation while reducing your prednisone needs, potentially decreasing long-term side effects.

Your Action Plan for Living Well with PMR

Daily Management Checklist

  1. Take medication as prescribed: Same time each day, with food if stomach upset occurs
  2. Stay active: Gentle exercise helps maintain muscle strength and bone health
  3. Eat well: Focus on calcium-rich foods, limit salt and sugar
  4. Monitor symptoms: Keep track of stiffness, pain levels, and any new symptoms
  5. Protect your bones: Take calcium and vitamin D supplements as recommended
  6. Get regular check-ups: Blood tests, blood pressure checks, bone density scans

When to Contact Me

Call immediately if you experience:
  • Sudden vision changes or vision loss
  • Severe headaches, especially temple pain
  • Jaw pain while chewing
  • Signs of serious infection (fever, chills, persistent cough)
Schedule an appointment if:
  • PMR symptoms return or worsen
  • Bothersome side effects from prednisone
  • Difficulty sleeping or mood changes
  • Any concerns about your treatment

The Outlook

Most people with PMR do very well with treatment. While the condition typically lasts 2-3 years, many people are able to eventually stop all medications and remain symptom-free. Some people may have a longer course, but with proper management, you can maintain a good quality of life.

Remember: PMR is very treatable. With the right medication, monitoring, and self-care, most people return to their normal activities and feel much better than before diagnosis.
