Understanding Anal Fissure

Prepared by your family doctor
What You Need to Know: Anal fissures are small tears in the lining of the anus that cause significant pain, especially during and after bowel movements. While uncomfortable, they are common and treatable with proper care.

What Is an Anal Fissure?

An anal fissure is a small tear or crack in the thin, moist tissue (mucosa) that lines the anus. Think of it like a paper cut in a very sensitive area. These tears typically occur in the back part of the anal opening and can cause sharp, burning pain during bowel movements.

Anal fissures are classified as:

  • Acute fissures: Recent tears that are shallow and have sharp edges
  • Chronic fissures: Deeper tears present for more than 8-12 weeks, often with exposed muscle fibers

Recognizing the Symptoms

Primary Symptoms

  • Sharp, tearing pain during bowel movements that can last for hours afterward
  • Bright red bleeding on toilet paper or in the toilet bowl
  • Visible tear in the skin around the anus
  • Itching or burning around the anal area

Secondary Symptoms

  • Muscle spasms around the anus
  • Small lump or skin tag near the fissure
  • Constipation due to fear of painful bowel movements
When to See Your Doctor Immediately: Seek medical attention if you experience severe bleeding, signs of infection (fever, pus), or if symptoms don't improve after 2-3 weeks of conservative treatment.

What Causes Anal Fissures?

Most anal fissures result from trauma to the anal canal during bowel movements. Common causes include:

Category Specific Causes
Bowel-related Hard, large stools; chronic diarrhea; straining during bowel movements
Medical conditions Crohn's disease, ulcerative colitis, previous anal surgery
Physical factors Childbirth, anal intercourse, prolonged sitting
Other factors Decreased blood flow to the area, muscle spasms

How We Diagnose Anal Fissures

Diagnosis is typically straightforward and involves:

Medical History

Your doctor will ask about your symptoms, bowel habits, and any recent changes in your routine or diet.

Physical Examination

A visual inspection of the anal area is usually sufficient to diagnose a fissure. The tear is often visible as a small crack in the skin. A digital rectal exam may be performed gently, though it's often too painful in acute cases.

Additional Tests (If Needed)

In some cases, your doctor may recommend:

  • Anoscopy (using a small scope to view the anal canal)
  • Flexible sigmoidoscopy (if inflammatory bowel disease is suspected)

Treatment Options

Most anal fissures heal with conservative treatment. The goal is to break the cycle of pain, spasm, and poor healing.

First-Line Conservative Treatment

Good News: About 90% of acute anal fissures heal completely with conservative treatment within 6-8 weeks.
  • Dietary modifications: Increase fiber intake to 25-30 grams daily
  • Hydration: Drink 8-10 glasses of water daily
  • Sitz baths: Warm water baths for 10-15 minutes, 2-3 times daily
  • Stool softeners: Over-the-counter options like docusate sodium
  • Pain management: Topical anesthetics or oral pain relievers as needed

Medical Treatment

For persistent or chronic fissures, your doctor may prescribe:

Prescription Treatment: In our practice, we commonly prescribe a combination cream containing Diltiazem 2% and Lidocaine 2% to be applied twice daily for 6 weeks. This medication helps relax the anal sphincter muscle and provides pain relief, allowing the fissure to heal.

How to use your prescribed cream:

  • Apply a small amount (pea-sized) to the anal area twice daily
  • Use clean hands or a glove for application
  • Apply gently around and just inside the anal opening
  • Continue for the full 6-week course, even after symptoms improve

When Surgery Might Be Needed

If conservative and medical treatments fail after 8-12 weeks, surgical options include:

  • Lateral internal sphincterotomy (most common)
  • Fissurectomy with or without flap advancement

Self-Care and Prevention

Daily Self-Care Checklist

  • □ Eat high-fiber foods (fruits, vegetables, whole grains)
  • □ Drink adequate water throughout the day
  • □ Take warm sitz baths after bowel movements
  • □ Use soft, unscented toilet paper
  • □ Apply prescribed medication as directed
  • □ Avoid straining during bowel movements
  • □ Don't delay when you feel the urge to have a bowel movement

Lifestyle Modifications

  • Exercise regularly: Physical activity promotes healthy bowel function
  • Establish a routine: Try to have bowel movements at the same time daily
  • Consider probiotics: May help improve overall digestive health
  • Manage stress: Stress can affect bowel habits and healing
Healing Mindset: Remember that healing takes time. Be patient with your body and consistent with treatment. Most people see significant improvement within 2-4 weeks of starting treatment.

What to Expect During Recovery

Healing typically follows this timeline:

  • Week 1-2: Pain reduction and decreased bleeding with treatment
  • Week 2-4: Continued improvement in symptoms
  • Week 4-6: Complete healing for most acute fissures
  • Beyond 6 weeks: Chronic fissures may need additional treatment
Red Flags - Call Your Doctor If:
  • Severe, worsening pain
  • Heavy bleeding or blood clots
  • Signs of infection (fever, pus, red streaking)
  • No improvement after 3 weeks of treatment
  • New lumps or changes around the anal area

Long-Term Outlook

With proper treatment, most anal fissures heal completely and don't return. However, some people may be prone to recurring fissures, especially if underlying risk factors (like chronic constipation) aren't addressed.

Key factors for preventing recurrence include:

  • Maintaining good bowel habits
  • Continuing high-fiber diet and adequate hydration
  • Prompt treatment of constipation
  • Regular follow-up with your healthcare provider if needed
