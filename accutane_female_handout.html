Isotretinoin (Accutane) for Acne

A Comprehensive Guide for Female Patients — Including the Pregnancy Prevention Program

⚠️ Critical Safety Information — Please Read First

Isotretinoin causes severe, life-threatening birth defects if taken during pregnancy. Every female patient of childbearing potential who takes this medication must follow a strict pregnancy prevention program throughout treatment and for one full month after the last dose. This is not optional — it is a medical requirement before your prescription can be filled each month.

Why Isotretinoin?

Isotretinoin — sold under the brand name Accutane, and available generically — is the most effective treatment available for severe or treatment-resistant acne. It is the only medication that can produce long-lasting or permanent remission of acne in many patients. Your doctor has recommended it because your acne has not responded adequately to other treatments, is causing or risking scarring, or is significantly affecting your quality of life.1,2

What Isotretinoin Can Do

Over 85% of patients achieve substantial or complete clearance of acne after a single course of isotretinoin.1 Many patients experience permanent remission — meaning the acne does not return. For those whose acne does recur, a second course is usually effective. No other acne treatment offers results that come close to this level of effectiveness.

How It Works

Isotretinoin is a derivative of vitamin A (a retinoid) that works through four key mechanisms simultaneously — which is why it is so much more effective than any single-target treatment:2,3

  • Dramatically reduces oil (sebum) production: It shrinks the skin's sebaceous (oil) glands by up to 90%, removing the environment in which acne-causing bacteria thrive.
  • Normalizes skin cell turnover inside pores: It prevents the abnormal shedding of cells that blocks pores and creates blackheads, whiteheads, and cysts.
  • Reduces inflammation: It decreases the inflammatory response in the skin that causes redness, swelling, and painful nodules.
  • Reduces acne bacteria indirectly: By reducing oil and altering the skin environment, it significantly lowers the population of Cutibacterium acnes bacteria without creating antibiotic resistance.
How Isotretinoin Targets Acne at Every Stage

Your Treatment Course

Dosing Based on Body Weight

Your total course dose of isotretinoin is calculated based on your body weight, with the target typically being 120 to 150 mg per kilogram of body weight delivered over the full course.2,4 Your doctor will start you on a lower dose and increase it gradually based on your response and tolerance. A typical course lasts 4 to 8 months, depending on your weight-based target dose and the dose you are able to tolerate.

Why Completing Your Course Matters

Reaching your full cumulative dose is strongly associated with achieving lasting remission and reducing the chance of acne returning.4 Stopping early because your skin looks clear — before the full dose is reached — significantly increases the likelihood of relapse. Clear skin is the goal, but completing the course is what makes that clearance last.

What to Expect Over Time

Typical Treatment Timeline
  • Weeks 1–4 (Initial adjustment): Your skin and lips will begin to feel dry as oil production drops. Some patients experience an initial flare of acne in the first 4 to 6 weeks — this is temporary and does not mean the medication is not working.
  • Months 1–2: Skin dryness and lip dryness typically peak. Oil production is dramatically reduced. New breakouts begin to diminish.
  • Months 2–4: Visible clearing accelerates. Existing blemishes resolve. Skin texture often improves significantly.
  • Months 4–8: Most patients achieve clear or nearly clear skin. Your dose is continued until you reach your total cumulative target.
  • After treatment: Skin continues to improve for up to 6 months after finishing the course as the skin heals fully.

The Pregnancy Prevention Program

⚠️ Isotretinoin and Pregnancy: Zero Tolerance for Exposure

Isotretinoin is classified as Pregnancy Category X in Canada — meaning it is absolutely contraindicated in pregnancy. Even a single dose taken during pregnancy can cause devastating birth defects. These include malformations of the brain, heart, face, ears, and limbs, as well as miscarriage and stillbirth. There is no safe dose of isotretinoin in pregnancy.5,6

Because of this, every female patient of childbearing potential who is prescribed isotretinoin must follow a formal pregnancy prevention program before, during, and for one month after treatment. Your prescription cannot be filled without documented compliance with this program.

Who Needs to Follow the Pregnancy Prevention Program?

The pregnancy prevention program applies to all female patients who have the potential to become pregnant. This includes patients who:5,6

  • Are sexually active with male partners
  • Are not currently sexually active but may become so during treatment
  • Have regular menstrual cycles

It does not apply only in very specific circumstances — for example, patients who have had a surgical hysterectomy or bilateral oophorectomy, or who have had premature menopause confirmed by a specialist. If you are unsure whether this applies to you, discuss it with your doctor.

Before Starting Isotretinoin

Before your very first prescription is issued, the following must occur:5,6,7

  • Two negative pregnancy tests — one done in the office at least 30 days before starting, and one done in the office immediately before the first prescription is written.
  • Contraception must already be in place — effective contraception must have been in use for at least one full month before treatment begins (where applicable — see below).
  • Counselling and consent — you will review and sign the informed consent document that outlines all risks and requirements. A copy will be kept in your chart and a copy given to you.
  • Baseline blood work — lipid panel, liver function tests, and CBC, as isotretinoin affects these values in some patients.

Two Forms of Contraception — or Confirmed Abstinence

There are two acceptable approaches to preventing pregnancy while on isotretinoin:

Option A — Two Concurrent Forms of Effective Contraception

If you are sexually active with male partners, or may become so, you must use two separate forms of effective contraception simultaneously throughout the entire course of treatment and for one full month after the last dose.5,6

One of the two methods should ideally be a highly effective "primary" method. The second is an additional backup. Recommended combinations include:

  • Oral contraceptive pill (OCP) plus condom
  • Hormonal IUD (e.g., Mirena) plus condom
  • Copper IUD plus condom
  • Contraceptive implant (Nexplanon) plus condom
  • Injectable contraceptive (Depo-Provera) plus condom
  • Contraceptive patch or ring plus condom

Note: Condoms alone are not considered sufficient as a primary contraceptive method for this program, due to the severity of risk if contraception fails.

Option B — Confirmed Abstinence

If you are absolutely certain you will not engage in sexual intercourse with a male partner at any point during the treatment course or for one month after the final dose, confirmed abstinence is accepted in place of contraception. This requires an honest and direct conversation with your doctor at each monthly visit. You must be certain — not just hopeful. If your situation changes at any point during treatment, you must notify your doctor immediately and arrange contraception before any sexual activity occurs.

If there is any uncertainty whatsoever about whether abstinence will be maintained throughout treatment, contraception is required. We recognize these conversations require candour, and your doctor will not judge you — the goal is simply to keep you safe.

Important: Isotretinoin May Reduce the Effectiveness of Progesterone-Only ("Mini") Pills

There is a theoretical interaction between isotretinoin and progesterone-only contraceptive pills (the "mini pill"). If you use this form of contraception, discuss it with your doctor before starting isotretinoin — a different or additional contraceptive method may be recommended.6

Monthly Office Visits — Required Before Every Prescription

Isotretinoin prescriptions for female patients of childbearing potential cannot be refilled without a monthly in-office visit. Each monthly visit must include:5,6,7

Pregnancy Test

A urine or serum pregnancy test is performed in the office. A negative result is required before the prescription is issued.

Contraception Review

Confirmation that effective contraception is in place and being used consistently, or abstinence is confirmed.

Clinical Assessment

Review of side effects, skin response, mood, and any new symptoms. Dose adjustment as needed.

Blood Work

Repeat fasting lipids and liver enzymes at prescribed intervals (typically months 1, 2, and then every 3 months).

If you miss a monthly appointment, your prescription cannot be renewed until the visit occurs and a negative pregnancy test is documented. Please plan your calendar carefully, as gaps in treatment are best avoided.

⚠️ If You Become Pregnant During Treatment — Act Immediately

Stop taking isotretinoin immediately. Do not take another dose. Contact your doctor the same day. You will need urgent referral to a teratology counselling service (a specialist service for medication exposure in pregnancy). Isotretinoin embryopathy — the pattern of birth defects caused by isotretinoin — is severe and occurs with even brief early exposure. The sooner you are seen, the more information you will have for decision-making.5,6

This situation is deeply distressing. Your healthcare team will not be judgemental — their concern is entirely for your health and wellbeing.

After Your Last Dose: One More Month of Contraception Required

Isotretinoin has a half-life that means it takes approximately one month to be fully cleared from your body after the final dose. Effective contraception must continue for a full 30 days after your last tablet.5 After this period, it is safe to attempt pregnancy if you wish. There is no evidence of any increased birth defect risk from isotretinoin taken prior to conception once the drug has cleared your system.

Side Effects and How to Manage Them

Common Side Effects (Expected by Almost All Patients)

Side Effect Why It Happens Management
Dry, chapped lips Sebaceous gland suppression reduces lipid film on lips Apply a rich lip balm (e.g., CeraVe Healing Ointment, Aquaphor, Vaseline) frequently — carry it everywhere. This is non-negotiable. Apply before bed as well.
Dry skin and face Reduced sebum production strips natural moisture barrier Gentle, fragrance-free moisturiser morning and night (e.g., CeraVe Moisturising Cream, La Roche-Posay Toleriane). Avoid harsh cleansers, scrubs, and toners.
Dry eyes Reduced Meibomian gland secretions Preservative-free artificial tears (e.g., Systane, Refresh). If you wear contact lenses, switch to glasses during treatment or at minimum reduce wear time. Discuss with your doctor if severe.
Dry nasal passages / nosebleeds Mucous membrane dryness Saline nasal spray or a small amount of petroleum jelly applied gently inside the nostrils. Avoid picking or blowing nose forcefully.
Increased sun sensitivity Thinning of the stratum corneum reduces UV protection Daily broad-spectrum SPF 30+ or higher sunscreen, even on cloudy days. Reapply if outdoors. Protective clothing and hats are important.
Initial acne flare Early inflammatory response as sebaceous glands react This is temporary and expected. It does not mean the medication is failing. Typically resolves within 4 to 6 weeks. Contact your doctor if it is severe.
Mild muscle aches Not fully understood; may relate to cartilage effects Reduce intense exercise during treatment if aching is significant. Acetaminophen (Tylenol) for comfort. Avoid NSAIDs (ibuprofen, naproxen) routinely as they affect platelets.
Hair thinning Telogen effluvium — temporary shift in hair growth cycle Usually mild and temporary. Hair regrows after treatment ends. Discuss with your doctor if shedding is significant or distressing.

Serious Side Effects — Know What to Watch For

Mood Changes and Mental Health

There is ongoing debate in the medical literature about isotretinoin and mood. Some patients report low mood, increased anxiety, or depression during treatment.8,9 It is not clearly established whether isotretinoin itself causes depression, or whether severe acne — which profoundly affects quality of life and self-esteem — is the primary driver of mood difficulties.

Regardless of the direction of causation, please contact your doctor promptly if you notice any of the following during treatment:

  • Persistent low mood or sadness
  • Increased irritability or anxiety
  • Feeling hopeless or worthless
  • Withdrawal from friends, family, or activities you normally enjoy
  • Any thoughts of self-harm

Your mental health matters as much as your skin health. Do not wait for your next scheduled appointment — contact us or present to urgent care if you are concerned.

Elevated Blood Lipids and Liver Enzymes

Isotretinoin can raise triglyceride and cholesterol levels and affect liver enzyme readings in some patients.2,10 This is why blood work is checked before treatment and at regular intervals during your course. These changes are generally reversible after treatment ends. Avoiding alcohol during treatment helps reduce liver risk and is strongly recommended.

Inflammatory Bowel Disease

There is a possible association between isotretinoin and inflammatory bowel disease (IBD), including Crohn's disease and ulcerative colitis, though evidence is not conclusive.11 If you develop significant abdominal pain, bloody stools, or persistent diarrhea during treatment, contact your doctor promptly.

Pseudotumour Cerebri (Benign Intracranial Hypertension)

Rarely, isotretinoin can cause increased pressure around the brain. Symptoms include severe headache (especially worse when lying down), visual changes or blurred vision, nausea, or ringing in the ears. Seek immediate medical attention if you experience these symptoms. This risk is increased if isotretinoin is taken with tetracycline-class antibiotics (doxycycline, minocycline) — these must not be combined.2,10

Your Skin Care Routine on Isotretinoin

A simplified, gentle routine is essential during treatment. Many skin care products that were fine before isotretinoin will now be too harsh. Your skin barrier becomes more delicate as oil production drops.12

Morning

  1. Cleanse with a mild, fragrance-free, non-foaming cleanser (e.g., CeraVe Hydrating Cleanser, La Roche-Posay Toleriane Hydrating Gentle Cleanser). Avoid cleansing brushes or cloths.
  2. Moisturise immediately after patting (not rubbing) dry with a rich, fragrance-free cream or lotion (e.g., CeraVe Moisturising Cream, Cetaphil Moisturising Cream).
  3. Sunscreen — a broad-spectrum SPF 30 or higher mineral or chemical sunscreen, every day without exception. Your skin burns more easily on isotretinoin.
  4. Lip balm — apply before going outside.

Evening

  1. Cleanse gently with the same mild cleanser.
  2. Moisturise generously.
  3. Lip balm or ointment — apply a thick layer before bed (Aquaphor or Vaseline work well overnight).

What to Avoid During Treatment

  • Do not use retinoids (tretinoin, retinol, adapalene) — combination would cause severe irritation.
  • Do not use exfoliating acids (AHAs, BHAs, glycolic acid, salicylic acid) — skin is too sensitive.
  • Avoid waxing any area of the face or body — skin tears easily and scarring is more likely. This includes professional waxing and home waxing strips.
  • Avoid dermabrasion or laser treatments during treatment and for at least 6 months after the course ends — scarring risk is elevated.
  • Minimise alcohol — it increases the risk of elevated liver enzymes and triglycerides.
  • Do not take vitamin A supplements — risk of vitamin A toxicity.
  • Do not take tetracycline antibiotics (doxycycline, minocycline) concurrently — risk of pseudotumour cerebri.

Makeup

Makeup is fine to wear during treatment. Look for products labelled "non-comedogenic" and "fragrance-free." You may find you need a more hydrating foundation or tinted moisturiser than before, as your skin will be drier than you are used to. Micellar water or a gentle oil-based makeup remover is kinder to skin than wipes.

Monthly Monitoring and Your Follow-Up Schedule

Isotretinoin requires structured monitoring throughout treatment. The following outlines what to expect at each stage:2,5,6

Visit When What Happens
Pre-treatment (Visit 1) ≥30 days before starting First pregnancy test, counselling, contraception plan, baseline blood work (lipids, liver enzymes, CBC), consent signed.
Pre-treatment (Visit 2) Immediately before starting Second pregnancy test (must be negative), confirm contraception in place, first prescription issued.
Month 1 4 weeks after starting Pregnancy test, contraception review, side effect assessment, blood work (lipids, LFTs), dose adjustment if needed.
Month 2 8 weeks after starting Pregnancy test, contraception review, side effect review, blood work, dose to full maintenance if tolerated.
Months 3–8 Monthly thereafter Pregnancy test every month, contraception confirmed every month, prescription issued only after these are done. Blood work every 3 months or as clinically indicated.
End of course At final visit Pregnancy test, final skin assessment, reminder about one month of continued contraception after last dose, post-treatment care plan discussed.
Post-treatment 1–3 months after finishing Skin review, assess need for maintenance therapy (e.g., topical retinoid), check if contraception has been continued for full 30 days after last dose.

A Note on Monthly Appointments

We understand that monthly visits are a significant commitment. These appointments are a non-negotiable component of safe prescribing for female patients on isotretinoin — not a bureaucratic formality. The pregnancy test requirement exists because even with perfect contraceptive use, no method is 100% reliable, and the consequences of fetal isotretinoin exposure are severe. Your safety, and your future reproductive health, drives every one of these requirements.

The Informed Consent Process

Before isotretinoin is prescribed, you will be asked to review and sign a separate Informed Consent and Pregnancy Prevention Agreement. This document is a legal and ethical requirement of prescribing isotretinoin to female patients of childbearing potential in Canada.5,6

What the Consent Form Covers

The informed consent document you will review includes:

  • Confirmation that you understand isotretinoin causes severe birth defects and must not be taken during pregnancy
  • Confirmation of your contraception plan or confirmed abstinence
  • Agreement to attend monthly office visits and undergo monthly pregnancy testing
  • Acknowledgement of all significant side effects, including mood effects, lipid changes, and musculoskeletal effects
  • Confirmation that you will contact your doctor immediately if you miss a period, think you may be pregnant, or have any unexpected sexual exposure
  • Your agreement not to share isotretinoin with anyone else
  • Acknowledgement that you will not donate blood during treatment or for one month after the last dose

A copy of the signed consent will be provided to you. Please keep it somewhere accessible.

Summary: What You Need to Remember

The Most Important Points

  • Isotretinoin is highly effective and can produce lasting acne clearance — completing your full course gives you the best chance of permanent remission.
  • Isotretinoin must never be taken during pregnancy. Use two forms of contraception, or be completely certain of abstinence throughout treatment and for one month after.
  • A pregnancy test is required every month in the office before your prescription can be refilled. Missing an appointment means a gap in treatment.
  • Dryness of the lips, skin, and eyes is almost universal — moisturise proactively and carry lip balm at all times.
  • Contact your doctor promptly if you notice mood changes, severe headaches, visual changes, abdominal symptoms, or signs of pregnancy.
  • Avoid waxing, exfoliants, retinoids, and vitamin A supplements during treatment.
  • Avoid alcohol during treatment to protect your liver.
  • Protect your skin from the sun every day with SPF 30+.

We Are Here to Support You

Starting isotretinoin is a significant commitment — but for many patients, it is the treatment that finally delivers the clear skin they have been working toward for years. The monitoring requirements, while demanding, exist to keep you safe. Your healthcare team is your partner in this process. Please do not hesitate to reach out between appointments if you have any concerns at all — no question is too small, and no symptom should be dismissed without asking.
