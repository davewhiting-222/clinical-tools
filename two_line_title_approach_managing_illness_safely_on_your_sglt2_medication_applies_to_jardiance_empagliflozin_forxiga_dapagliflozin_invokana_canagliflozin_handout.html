Managing Illness Safely on Your SGLT2 Medication

Applies to: Jardiance (empagliflozin), Forxiga (dapagliflozin), Invokana (canagliflozin)
December 2024

Know Your Medication

This guide applies to a class of medications called SGLT2 inhibitors. These medications are excellent for protecting your heart and kidneys while helping control blood sugar. You may be taking one of the following:

Generic Name Brand Name Days to Stop Before Surgery
Empagliflozin Jardiance 3 days
Dapagliflozin Forxiga 3 days
Canagliflozin Invokana 3 days
Ertugliflozin Steglatro 4 days

Why Special Rules Are Necessary

While SGLT2 inhibitors are very safe for daily use, they carry a unique risk during illness, fasting, or surgery. When combined with dehydration or not eating normally, these medications can trigger a serious condition called diabetic ketoacidosis (DKA).

The Hidden Danger

The most dangerous aspect is that your blood sugar may stay completely normal while your blood becomes dangerously acidic. This can mask the emergency, which is why following these rules is critical for your safety.

The SADMANS Rule for Illness

Use this memory aid to remember when to stop your SGLT2 inhibitor:

SADMANS - Stop Your Medication If You Have:

  • Surgery (planned procedure)
  • Alcohol excess
  • Dehydration
  • Malaise (feeling unwell)
  • Anesthesia (upcoming procedure)
  • Nausea or vomiting
  • Sick with fever, flu, or diarrhea

When to Stop During Illness

Immediately stop taking your SGLT2 inhibitor if you develop:

  • Flu symptoms or fever
  • Vomiting or diarrhea
  • Any illness causing dehydration
  • Inability to eat or drink normally

When to Restart After Illness

Only restart your medication when:

  • You feel completely well
  • You've been eating and drinking normally for 24 hours
  • You're staying well-hydrated

The SSTOP Protocol for Procedures

Fasting for medical procedures creates the same risks as illness. You must plan ahead:

Before Any Procedure Requiring Fasting

  • Jardiance, Forxiga, Invokana: Stop 3 days before
  • Steglatro (ertugliflozin): Stop 4 days before
  • This includes surgery, colonoscopy, or any procedure requiring you not to eat
  • Inform your surgeon and anesthesiologist about this medication

Emergency Warning Signs

Go to Emergency Immediately If You Experience:

  • Nausea or vomiting (especially with normal blood sugar)
  • Abdominal pain
  • Extreme fatigue or weakness
  • Shortness of breath or rapid breathing
  • Feeling very unwell despite normal glucose readings

Golden Rule: If you feel nauseous and sick but your blood sugar is normal, assume this could be a drug-related emergency until proven otherwise.

Key Messages to Remember

  • SGLT2 inhibitors are excellent medications when used properly
  • Following sick day rules prevents serious complications
  • When in doubt, stop the medication and contact your healthcare team
  • Always inform healthcare providers that you take an SGLT2 inhibitor
  • Normal blood sugar during illness doesn't mean you're safe if taking these medications
