Taking Birth Control Continuously to Avoid Menstruation

Prepared by your family doctor
The Bottom Line: Taking birth control continuously (skipping the placebo or "sugar" pills) is an evidence-based practice supported by major gynaecological societies. The monthly cycle was designed in the 1960s for social acceptance, not medical necessity.

Why Consider Continuous Birth Control?

There are both therapeutic and lifestyle reasons why your doctor might recommend skipping the withdrawal bleed (the bleeding that occurs during the placebo week).

Symptom Management Benefits

Continuous use can help manage "menstrual-related exacerbations" including:

  • Dysmenorrhea: Severe menstrual cramping
  • Menstrual Migraines: Headaches triggered by the drop in estrogen during the placebo week
  • Endometriosis/Adenomyosis: Reducing bleeding helps manage pain from these conditions
  • Heavy Menstrual Bleeding: Preventing blood loss in patients with anemia
  • PMDD: Stabilizing hormone levels can reduce severe mood fluctuations

Quality of Life Benefits

  • Avoid the logistical burden and physical discomfort of monthly bleeding
  • Increased contraceptive effectiveness - if you miss a pill, the risk of ovulation is lower because your ovaries have been suppressed more consistently
  • Freedom from menstrual hygiene requirements during travel, sports, or special events

How to Take Birth Control Continuously

The method depends on the type of contraceptive you're using. Monophasic pills (where every active pill contains the same dose) work best for this approach.

Combined Oral Contraceptives (Birth Control Pills)

The Method: Finish the 21 active pills in your pack. Instead of taking the 7 placebo pills, throw them away and start the first active pill of a new pack the very next day.

Best Options: Monophasic pills like Ovima or Alysena work most smoothly. Triphasic pills (like Linessa) change doses weekly and are more likely to cause breakthrough bleeding during the transition between packs.

Other Methods

Method How to Use Continuously
Vaginal Ring (e.g., Haloette) Remove the old ring after 21 days and immediately insert a new one, or leave the ring in for 4 weeks and swap immediately
Contraceptive Patch Apply a new patch every week without a "patch-free" week

Common Concerns and What to Expect

Breakthrough Bleeding

Most Common Issue: This is the #1 reason people stop continuous birth control. Your uterine lining may become unstable and "shed" small amounts at unpredictable times. This usually improves after 3-6 months of continuous use.

Other Considerations

Concern Explanation
Hormonal Exposure You receive a slightly higher total annual dose of hormones. For most healthy individuals, this doesn't increase serious complication risks beyond the baseline risk of the pill itself.
Masking Pregnancy Without monthly bleeding, you might not realize if the method fails. Consider routine pregnancy tests every few months if this concerns you.
Cost/Supply in BC Since birth control is now free in BC, ensure your prescription specifies "continuous use" so your pharmacist can dispense packs frequently enough.

The "Safety Valve" Approach

If breakthrough bleeding becomes bothersome (lasting 3-4+ days): Take a 3-4 day break from your pills (provided you've taken at least 21 days of active pills consecutively) to allow a controlled bleed, then resume continuous use. This "resets" your uterine lining.

Safety and Peace of Mind

Important Facts:

  • There's no evidence that the uterine lining "builds up" dangerously during continuous use
  • The progestogens in birth control keep the lining thin and inactive
  • When you stop the medication, the lining will shed normally
  • Fertility typically returns within the same timeframe as with cycled use

Quick Start Checklist

  1. Discuss with your doctor whether continuous birth control is right for you
  2. Choose a monophasic pill if possible (Ovima, Alysena)
  3. Ensure your prescription specifies "continuous use"
  4. Skip the placebo pills and start your new pack immediately
  5. Expect some breakthrough bleeding in the first 3-6 months
  6. Consider the "safety valve" approach if bleeding becomes bothersome
  7. Schedule regular check-ups with your healthcare provider
When to Contact Your Doctor: Heavy or prolonged breakthrough bleeding, severe side effects, signs of pregnancy, or any concerns about your contraceptive method.
