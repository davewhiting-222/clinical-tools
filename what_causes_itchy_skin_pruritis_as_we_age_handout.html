What Causes Itchy Skin (Pruritus) As We Age

Prepared by your family doctor
December 2024
Why This Matters: Itchy skin affects up to 85% of older adults and can significantly impact sleep and quality of life. Understanding the causes helps us find the right treatment approach for you.

What Is Age-Related Pruritus?

Pruritus (medical term for itching) becomes increasingly common as we age. Your skin changes naturally over time, becoming drier and more sensitive. This creates the perfect conditions for persistent itching that can be both frustrating and disruptive to daily life.1

Common Causes of Itchy Skin in Older Adults

1. Dry Skin (Xerosis) - The Most Common Cause

As we age, our skin produces less natural oil and loses moisture more easily. This leads to:

  • Rough, flaky skin texture
  • Fine cracks in the skin surface
  • Increased sensitivity to environmental factors
  • More pronounced itching, especially in winter

2. Skin Changes From Aging

Natural aging processes affect your skin's ability to maintain itself:

  • Thinning of the outer skin layer
  • Reduced production of natural moisturizing factors
  • Slower skin cell turnover
  • Decreased blood flow to the skin

3. Environmental and Lifestyle Factors

  • Low humidity: Particularly during Canadian winters with indoor heating
  • Hot showers or baths: Strip natural oils from skin
  • Harsh soaps: Disrupt the skin's protective barrier
  • Certain fabrics: Wool or synthetic materials can irritate sensitive skin

Medical Conditions That Can Cause Itching

Important: While dry skin is the most common cause, persistent itching can sometimes signal underlying medical conditions that need evaluation.

Common Medical Causes Include:

Condition Additional Signs to Watch For
Eczema (Atopic Dermatitis) Red, inflamed patches; skin thickening in scratched areas
Contact Dermatitis Itching in specific areas exposed to irritants or allergens
Kidney Disease Swelling, changes in urination, fatigue
Liver Disease Yellowing of skin/eyes, abdominal swelling
Thyroid Disorders Weight changes, temperature sensitivity, mood changes
Diabetes Increased thirst/urination, slow wound healing
Medication Side Effects Itching that started after beginning new medication

Effective Management Strategies

Good News: Most age-related itching responds well to proper skin care and lifestyle adjustments. Here's what works best.

Daily Skin Care Routine

  1. Gentle Cleansing: Use lukewarm water and fragrance-free, moisturizing cleansers like Cetaphil or CeraVe
  2. Pat Dry: Don't rub your skin dry - pat gently with a soft towel
  3. Immediate Moisturizing: Apply moisturizer within 3 minutes of bathing while skin is still damp
  4. Choose the Right Products: Look for thick creams or ointments rather than thin lotions

Recommended Moisturizers (Available in Canada)

  • ✓ Eucerin Advanced Repair
  • ✓ CeraVe Moisturizing Cream
  • ✓ Aveeno Daily Moisturizing Lotion
  • ✓ Vaseline (petroleum jelly) for very dry areas
  • ✓ Glaxal Base (available at Canadian pharmacies)

Environmental Modifications

  • Humidity: Use a humidifier to maintain 30-50% humidity, especially in winter
  • Temperature: Keep your home comfortably cool to reduce sweating and irritation
  • Clothing: Choose soft, natural fabrics like cotton; avoid wool directly on skin
  • Laundry: Use fragrance-free, hypoallergenic detergents

Bathing Best Practices

  • Limit baths/showers to 10 minutes
  • Use lukewarm (not hot) water
  • Add colloidal oatmeal or baking soda to baths for soothing relief
  • Avoid bubble baths and harsh scrubbing

When to Seek Medical Attention

Red Flags - See Your Doctor Urgently If You Have:

  • Sudden, severe itching all over your body
  • Itching with fever, weight loss, or night sweats
  • Yellow discoloration of skin or eyes
  • Significant swelling of legs, ankles, or abdomen
  • Open sores or signs of infection from scratching
  • Itching that prevents sleep or interferes with daily activities

Schedule a Regular Appointment If:

  • Itching persists despite 2-3 weeks of proper skin care
  • You need help identifying potential irritants or allergens
  • Over-the-counter treatments aren't providing relief
  • You have other symptoms that concern you

Treatment Options Your Doctor Might Recommend

Topical Treatments

  • Prescription moisturizers: Medical-grade barrier repair creams
  • Topical corticosteroids: For inflamed, itchy areas (short-term use)
  • Calcineurin inhibitors: For sensitive areas like face and neck
  • Menthol or camphor lotions: For cooling, anti-itch relief

Oral Medications

  • Antihistamines: Especially helpful if allergies are involved
  • Other medications: For specific underlying conditions
Remember: Managing itchy skin as we age requires patience and consistency. Most people see significant improvement within 2-4 weeks of starting a proper skincare routine. Don't hesitate to work with your healthcare team to find what works best for you.
