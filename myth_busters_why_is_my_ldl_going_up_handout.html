MYTH-BUSTERS: Why is my LDL going up?

Prepared by your family doctor December 2024

You've been eating healthier, maybe even following a low-carb or ketogenic diet, and your LDL cholesterol has gone up. What's happening? Let's bust some myths and explore what your numbers really mean.

The Big Picture: LDL cholesterol going up doesn't automatically mean your heart disease risk is increasing. The story is much more nuanced than total LDL numbers alone.

Understanding LDL: It's Not Just One Thing

LDL Particle Size Matters

Think of LDL as delivery trucks carrying cholesterol through your bloodstream. There are two main types:

  • Large, fluffy LDL particles: Like big delivery trucks that bounce off artery walls
  • Small, dense LDL particles: Like small, sharp trucks that can get stuck in artery walls
Key Insight: Small, dense LDL particles are the ones associated with heart disease risk. Large, fluffy particles may actually be protective. Standard cholesterol tests don't distinguish between these types.

The Triglyceride to HDL Ratio: A Better Crystal Ball

Canadian research shows that your triglyceride-to-HDL ratio is often a better predictor of heart disease risk than LDL alone.1

TG/HDL Ratio Risk Level What It Suggests
Less than 2:1 Low Risk Likely large, fluffy LDL particles
2:1 to 4:1 Moderate Risk Mixed particle sizes
Greater than 4:1 Higher Risk Likely small, dense LDL particles

Why LDL Goes Up on Healthy Diets

The Ketogenic Diet Effect

Many people see their LDL rise when starting a ketogenic or very low-carb diet. Here's why this might happen:

  1. Increased fat mobilization: Your body is breaking down stored fat for energy
  2. Liver cholesterol production: Your liver may temporarily increase cholesterol production
  3. Improved cholesterol transport: More efficient movement of cholesterol through your system
  4. Particle size shift: Often, you're making more large, fluffy particles
Good News: Studies show that even when LDL rises on ketogenic diets, people often see improvements in other heart health markers like blood pressure, inflammation, and insulin sensitivity.2

Other Reasons for Rising LDL

  • Weight loss phase: Active fat loss can temporarily raise cholesterol
  • Thyroid function: Underactive thyroid can increase LDL
  • Genetics: Some people naturally run higher cholesterol levels
  • Seasonal changes: LDL can vary by 20% seasonally
  • Recent illness or stress: Can temporarily affect cholesterol levels

When to Worry vs. When Not to Worry

Be Concerned If:
  • Your triglycerides are high (over 2.0 mmol/L) AND HDL is low (under 1.0 for men, 1.3 for women)
  • You have multiple heart disease risk factors (diabetes, high blood pressure, smoking)
  • You have a strong family history of early heart disease
  • Your LDL is extremely high (over 5.0 mmol/L) with other concerning factors
Less Concerning When:
  • Your triglycerides are low (under 1.0 mmol/L) and HDL is high (over 1.5 mmol/L)
  • You're actively losing weight or recently changed your diet
  • You have no other heart disease risk factors
  • Your blood pressure and blood sugar are well-controlled

Better Markers to Track

Beyond Basic Cholesterol

Ask your doctor about these additional tests that give a clearer picture:

  • ApoB (Apolipoprotein B): Counts actual LDL particles, not just cholesterol content
  • Lp(a) (Lipoprotein little a): An independent genetic risk factor
  • hs-CRP (high-sensitivity C-reactive protein): Measures inflammation
  • Insulin levels: High insulin drives small, dense LDL production
  • HbA1c: Long-term blood sugar control affects particle size

Lifestyle Markers That Matter

  • Blood pressure trends
  • Waist circumference
  • Energy levels and sleep quality
  • Exercise capacity
  • Mood and mental clarity

Action Steps

What You Can Do:
  1. Calculate your TG/HDL ratio from your last bloodwork
  2. Track trends, not single values - cholesterol can fluctuate
  3. Focus on the whole picture - blood pressure, weight, energy
  4. Discuss advanced testing with your doctor if concerned
  5. Continue healthy lifestyle habits - they're working even if LDL is higher
Important: Never stop prescribed cholesterol medications without discussing with your doctor. If you're concerned about rising LDL, book an appointment to discuss your individual risk factors and whether additional testing or treatment changes are needed.

The Bottom Line

Rising LDL isn't automatically bad news, especially if you're making positive lifestyle changes. Your heart health depends on many factors beyond total LDL cholesterol. Focus on the quality of your LDL particles, your overall metabolic health, and work with your healthcare team to understand your individual risk profile.

Remember: Heart disease prevention is about the long game. Sustainable lifestyle changes that improve your overall metabolic health are more important than chasing perfect cholesterol numbers.
