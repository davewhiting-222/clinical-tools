Electrolyte Replacement on a Ketogenic Diet

Prepared by your family doctor December 2024

Why This Matters: When you start a ketogenic diet, your body rapidly loses sodium and water. This natural process also causes your body to waste potassium and magnesium. The resulting electrolyte imbalance causes the temporary symptoms known as "keto flu" - but these are completely preventable with proper mineral replacement.

Understanding the "Keto Flu"

When you dramatically reduce carbohydrates, your insulin levels drop. This signals your kidneys to rapidly release stored sodium and water - a normal process called "natriuresis of fasting." As sodium is flushed out, your body also begins losing potassium and magnesium to maintain balance.

This mineral loss creates symptoms like:

  • Fatigue and brain fog
  • Headaches and dizziness
  • Nausea and digestive upset
  • Muscle cramps and weakness
  • Heart palpitations (racing or irregular heartbeat)
  • Constipation

Good News: These symptoms are temporary and completely preventable by replacing electrolytes properly. Most people feel dramatically better within 24-48 hours of adequate mineral replacement.

Your Daily Electrolyte Targets

Mineral Daily Target Why It's Important
Sodium 3,000-5,000 mg Foundation mineral - if sodium is low, potassium continues to be wasted
Potassium 2,000-4,000 mg Essential for nerve function, heart rhythm, and muscle contractions
Magnesium 300-500 mg Prevents muscle cramps, supports sleep, and helps with stress

1. Sodium (The Foundation)

Sodium is the most critical mineral to manage. Without adequate sodium, your body will continue wasting potassium no matter how much you consume.

Best Sources:

  • High-quality sea salt (add to food liberally)
  • 1-2 cups of salty bone broth or bouillon daily
  • Pickles and olives
  • Salted nuts and seeds

The "Salt Test": If you feel a headache or dizziness coming on, place a pinch of salt under your tongue. If symptoms improve within 15 minutes, you need more sodium. This simple test can help you recognize when you're under-salted.

2. Potassium (For Heart and Muscles)

Potassium deficiency can cause muscle spasms, constipation, and heart palpitations.

Best Sources:

  • Avocados (1 medium = 975mg)
  • Spinach and Swiss chard
  • Salmon and other fish
  • Mushrooms and Brussels sprouts
  • Salt substitutes like "Lite-Salt" or "No-Salt" (potassium chloride)

Important: Avoid high-dose potassium supplements unless prescribed by your doctor. Too much potassium taken at once can be dangerous for your heart. Focus on dietary sources and salt substitutes spread throughout the day.

3. Magnesium (For Sleep and Cramps)

Magnesium deficiency is common and causes leg cramps, poor sleep, and increased stress sensitivity.

Best Food Sources:

  • Pumpkin seeds (1 oz = 150mg)
  • Almonds and cashews
  • Dark chocolate (85% cacao or higher)
  • Leafy greens

Supplement Forms (if needed):

  • Best: Magnesium Glycinate, Magnesium Malate, or slow-release Magnesium Chloride
  • Avoid: Magnesium Oxide (poorly absorbed and mainly acts as a laxative)
  • Timing: Take in the evening to improve sleep quality

The Daily "Keto-Ade" Recipe

Instead of drinking plain water (which can further dilute your electrolytes), sip this electrolyte drink throughout the day to maintain proper mineral balance.

Basic Keto-Ade Recipe

Mix the following in 1 litre of filtered water:

  • 1/2 teaspoon high-quality sea salt (provides ~1,100mg sodium)
  • 1/4 teaspoon potassium chloride (like "Lite-Salt")
  • Optional: Squeeze of lemon or lime juice
  • Optional: A few drops of stevia for sweetness

Instructions: Sip throughout the day rather than drinking all at once. Make 1-2 batches daily depending on your activity level and climate.

Why Commercial Sports Drinks Don't Work: Popular "zero sugar" sports drinks only contain 50-150mg of sodium per serving - far less than the 3,000-5,000mg you need daily on a ketogenic diet. They're designed for people eating high-carb diets who naturally retain more sodium.

When to Contact Your Healthcare Provider

Immediate Medical Attention Required If You Have:

  • Blood pressure medications: Diuretics (water pills), ACE inhibitors, or ARBs can interact dangerously with the natural diuretic effect of ketosis
  • Chronic kidney disease: Your kidneys may not handle increased mineral loads safely
  • SGLT2 inhibitors for diabetes (Jardiance, Farxiga, Invokana): Risk of a serious condition called euglycemic diabetic ketoacidosis
  • Heart conditions: Rapid electrolyte changes can affect heart rhythm

Seek Emergency Care If You Experience:

  • Severe, persistent abdominal pain
  • Chest pain or shortness of breath
  • Extreme muscle weakness that doesn't improve with electrolyte replacement
  • Nausea, vomiting, and rapid breathing (especially if taking diabetes medications)
  • Severe dizziness or fainting

Your Action Plan

Week 1-2 Checklist:

  1. Start each day with a cup of salty bone broth
  2. Add extra salt to all your meals
  3. Prepare 1-2 litres of "keto-ade" daily and sip throughout the day
  4. Take magnesium supplement in the evening
  5. Include potassium-rich foods like avocados and leafy greens
  6. Monitor how you feel and adjust sodium intake based on symptoms
  7. Contact your doctor if you take any medications listed above

Signs You're Getting Enough Electrolytes

  • Stable energy throughout the day
  • No headaches or dizziness
  • Good sleep quality
  • No muscle cramps
  • Regular bowel movements
  • Clear thinking and good mood

Remember: The transition period typically lasts 1-4 weeks. Your body is learning to use fat for fuel instead of sugar - this is a major metabolic shift. Be patient with yourself and focus on consistent electrolyte replacement. Most people feel significantly better by week 2-3.
