MYTH-BUSTERS: Is it Safe to Eat Low-Carb with Diabetes?

Prepared by your family doctor
December 2024

The Bottom Line: Low-carbohydrate diets can be safe and highly effective for managing diabetes when done properly with medical supervision. The key is understanding how to adjust medications and monitor your blood sugars safely.

The Science: How Low-Carb Diets Help Diabetes

When you eat fewer carbohydrates, your blood sugar naturally stays more stable. Here's what happens in your body:

  • Less glucose enters your bloodstream because carbs break down into sugar
  • Your body needs less insulin to handle the smaller amount of sugar
  • Your cells become more sensitive to insulin over time
  • Your liver produces less glucose when not constantly dealing with dietary carbs

Research Results: The landmark Virta Health study showed that 60% of people with type 2 diabetes achieved remission (normal blood sugars without medication) after one year on a medically supervised low-carb diet.¹

Why Medication Adjustment is Critical

Here's the most important safety point: if you take diabetes medications, especially insulin or sulfonylureas (glyburide, gliclazide), your doses may need to be reduced quickly when you start eating low-carb.

Warning: Taking your usual diabetes medication doses while eating very few carbs can cause dangerous low blood sugar (hypoglycemia). This is why medical supervision is essential.

Medications That May Need Adjustment

Medication Type Examples Risk Level
Insulin All types HIGH - requires immediate adjustment
Sulfonylureas Glyburide, gliclazide HIGH - may cause low blood sugar
Metformin Glucophage LOW - usually safe to continue
SGLT2 inhibitors Empagliflozin, canagliflozin MODERATE - monitor for ketones

Essential Monitoring Requirements

When starting a low-carb diet with diabetes, more frequent monitoring helps ensure your safety:

Daily Monitoring Checklist

  • Check blood glucose 4+ times daily for the first few weeks
  • Test for ketones if blood sugar is above 14 mmol/L
  • Record your carb intake and blood sugar patterns
  • Watch for symptoms of low blood sugar
  • Stay well hydrated (8+ glasses of water daily)

Signs to Watch For

Call your doctor immediately if you experience:

  • Blood sugar below 4 mmol/L repeatedly
  • Symptoms of low blood sugar: shaking, sweating, confusion, rapid heartbeat
  • High ketones with nausea or vomiting
  • Persistent blood sugars above 20 mmol/L

The Research Evidence

Multiple high-quality studies have demonstrated the safety and effectiveness of low-carb diets for diabetes:

  • Virta Health Study: 262 adults with type 2 diabetes followed for 2 years showed sustained improvements in blood sugar control and significant medication reductions²
  • Duke University Research: Participants reduced HbA1c by 1.5% and lost an average of 11 kg over 6 months³
  • Systematic Reviews: Analysis of 23 studies confirmed low-carb diets are more effective than low-fat diets for diabetes management⁴

Good News: When done with proper medical supervision, low-carb diets have excellent safety records and can lead to dramatic improvements in diabetes control, often allowing people to reduce or eliminate medications.

Getting Started Safely

The safest approach is a gradual, medically supervised transition:

  1. Consult your healthcare team first - never start without medical guidance
  2. Begin with moderate carb reduction (100-150g daily) before going very low-carb
  3. Adjust medications proactively as your doctor recommends
  4. Monitor blood sugars frequently for the first month
  5. Stay connected with your healthcare team for ongoing adjustments

Remember: The goal isn't just lower blood sugars - it's achieving better overall health while staying safe. With proper medical supervision, low-carb eating can be a powerful tool for managing diabetes.
