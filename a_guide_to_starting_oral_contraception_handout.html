A Guide to Starting Oral Contraception

Prepared by your family doctor

April 16, 2026

Key Message: Starting birth control pills is straightforward and safe for most people. This guide will help you understand what to expect and how to use them effectively.

What Happens at Your First Visit

When you come in to discuss birth control pills, we'll focus on making sure they're safe for you. Here's what to expect:

Essential Health Checks

  • Medical history review: We'll ask about any conditions that might make the pill unsuitable (like severe migraines, blood clots, or liver problems)
  • Blood pressure check: This is required before starting any combined pill and will be checked annually
  • Pregnancy test: To confirm you're not currently pregnant

Good news: You don't need a pelvic exam, Pap test, or breast exam to start birth control pills if you're healthy and have no symptoms.

Choosing the Right Pill for You

There are many options, but we typically start with a low-dose pill that's easy to use and monitor.

Estrogen Dose Options

Dose Best For Benefits
30-35 mcg First-time users Better cycle control, less breakthrough bleeding
20 mcg Those sensitive to estrogen Fewer estrogen-related side effects like nausea

Progestogen Type

We often recommend levonorgestrel (a second-generation progestogen) for first-time users because it has a slightly lower risk of blood clots compared to newer types.

Why monophasic pills? These give you the same hormone dose every day, making them easier to manage if you miss a pill and allowing for flexible timing if you want to skip periods later.

How to Start Your Pills

We recommend the "Quick Start" method because it's most effective at preventing pregnancy:

Method Instructions Backup Needed
Quick Start (Recommended) Start the first pill today or when you pick up your prescription Use condoms for 7 days
First Day Start Start on the first day of your next period No backup needed
Sunday Start Start on the first Sunday after your period begins Use condoms for 7 days

What to Expect and Important Safety Information

Common Early Side Effects

Breakthrough bleeding: Spotting between periods is very common in the first 3 months. This is normal and doesn't mean the pill isn't working. It usually stops on its own.

Other possible side effects in the first few months include:

  • Mild nausea (try taking the pill with food or at bedtime)
  • Breast tenderness
  • Mood changes
  • Headaches

When to Seek Immediate Medical Help - Remember "ACHES"

Call your doctor or go to emergency if you have:

  • Abdominal pain (severe)
  • Chest pain or shortness of breath
  • Headaches (severe or new type)
  • Eye problems (blurred vision, flashing lights)
  • Severe leg pain or swelling

If You Miss a Pill

Quick Reference Guide

  • 1 pill missed: Take it as soon as you remember, even if you take two pills in one day
  • 2+ pills missed: Take the most recent missed pill, discard the others, use backup contraception for 7 days
  • Missed pills in the last week of the pack: Skip the hormone-free week and start a new pack right away

Protection Against STIs

Remember: Birth control pills prevent pregnancy but don't protect against sexually transmitted infections (STIs). Using condoms along with the pill ("double-dutching") gives you the best protection against both pregnancy and STIs.

Your Follow-Up Care

We'll schedule a check-in at 3 months to:

  • Check your blood pressure
  • See how you're adjusting to the pill
  • Address any side effects or concerns
  • Make sure you're happy with your choice

After that, annual visits are usually sufficient unless you have concerns.

You're in control: If this pill doesn't work well for you, we have many other options. Don't hesitate to reach out if you're having problems - we can almost always find a solution that works better.
