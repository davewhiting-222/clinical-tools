Understanding My Carbohydrate Targets

Prepared by your family doctor
December 19, 2024
Your portal shows a carb target number — this handout explains what that means for your daily meals and snacks.

What is a "Net Carb"?

Net carbs are the carbs that actually affect your blood sugar. Here's the simple formula:

Net Carbs = Total Carbs - Fibre
Fibre doesn't count because your body can't digest it into sugar.

For example, one cup of broccoli has 6g total carbs and 2g fibre, so it's only 4g net carbs. This is why you can eat plenty of high-fibre vegetables without worrying about your carb target.

What Does Your Target Mean?

Your portal shows one of these targets based on your health goals:

Why This Target Was Chosen for You

Your carb target wasn't picked randomly. Your nurse and physician considered:

  • Your current health conditions (like diabetes or pre-diabetes)
  • Your medications and how they work with different carb levels
  • Your personal health goals
  • What's realistic and sustainable for your lifestyle
Remember: This target is personalized medical guidance, not a one-size-fits-all diet rule.

How to Distribute Carbs Across Your Day

A good rule of thumb is to spread your carbs roughly equally across three meals:

  • Breakfast: About 1/3 of your daily target
  • Lunch: About 1/3 of your daily target
  • Dinner: About 1/3 of your daily target
  • Snacks: Count toward your total — nothing is "free"
Example: If your target is 90g net carbs per day, aim for about 30g at each meal. A small snack would mean slightly less at meals.

Practical Portion Guide

Here's what common foods look like in carb terms:

1 slice bread ~15g net carbs
1 cup cooked rice ~45g net carbs
1 medium apple ~25g net carbs
1 cup pasta ~40g net carbs
1 cup broccoli ~4g net carbs
½ cup berries ~6g net carbs
1 medium potato ~35g net carbs
2 cups leafy greens ~2g net carbs

High-Fibre Foods You Can Eat Freely

These foods are so low in net carbs that you don't need to worry about portions:

  • Leafy greens (spinach, lettuce, kale)
  • Cucumbers and celery
  • Bell peppers and zucchini
  • Asparagus and green beans
  • Cauliflower and cabbage

What Happens If You Go Over Your Target?

One day over your target doesn't undo your progress. What matters is the pattern over time, not perfection every single day.

If you go over your target:

  • Don't stress — just get back on track with your next meal
  • Notice what led to going over (was it a social event? skipped meal? stress eating?)
  • Use it as learning for next time
  • Focus on the positive changes you're making overall
If you're on diabetes medications: Going significantly over your carb target can affect your blood sugar. Monitor more closely and contact us if you have concerns.

If You're on the Ketogenic Pathway

Keto patients start with a higher target that steps down gradually over several weeks. Your portal always shows your current target for this week. Don't worry about the final keto goal yet — just focus on today's number.

Keto patients: Refer to your separate "Ketogenic Diet Guide" for detailed stepping instructions and what to expect during the transition.

Quick Reference Checklist

Daily Success Checklist:
  • Check my carb target on the portal
  • Plan roughly equal carbs at each meal
  • Count net carbs (total carbs minus fibre)
  • Fill half my plate with low-carb vegetables
  • Track my intake if recommended
  • Stay hydrated and take my medications as prescribed
