Nattokinase - What Is It, and Does It Work?

Prepared by your family doctor
December 2024

What Is Nattokinase?

Nattokinase is an enzyme found in natto, a traditional Japanese fermented soybean dish. This enzyme is created during the fermentation process by bacteria called Bacillus subtilis. Many people take nattokinase as a supplement, hoping it will improve heart health and circulation.

Key Point: Nattokinase is a natural enzyme that breaks down blood clots. It works differently from prescription blood thinners like warfarin, acting more like your body's natural clot-dissolving system.

How Is Nattokinase Supposed to Work?

Your body has a natural system to break down blood clots when they're no longer needed. Nattokinase appears to help this process by:

  • Breaking down fibrin (the protein that forms blood clots)
  • Helping your body's own clot-dissolving enzymes work better
  • Potentially reducing substances that make blood clots stick around longer

What Does the Research Actually Show?

The evidence for nattokinase is mixed and limited. Here's what we know from studies:

Promising Areas

Blood Pressure: A study of 86 people found that taking nattokinase for 8 weeks lowered blood pressure by about 5.5 mmHg systolic and 2.8 mmHg diastolic compared to placebo. While statistically significant, this is a modest effect.1

Disappointing Results

Heart Disease Prevention: The largest and longest study (NAPS trial) followed 265 people for 3 years. Nattokinase did not prevent atherosclerosis (hardening of arteries) compared to placebo.2 This was the most important test, and nattokinase failed it.

Four Big Problems With Nattokinase

1. Absorption Issues

Nattokinase is a large protein that may be destroyed by stomach acid before it reaches your bloodstream. Studies show conflicting results about whether intact, active nattokinase actually gets absorbed. Some research suggests the enzyme peaks in blood 13 hours after taking it, but the effects on blood clotting peak in only 2-4 hours. This mismatch suggests we may not be measuring what we think we're measuring.

2. Dosing Confusion

Nattokinase is measured in "fibrinolytic units" (FU), not just milligrams. A product labeled "100mg nattokinase" tells you nothing about how much active enzyme you're getting. Most studies use 2,000 FU daily, but supplement labels are inconsistent and confusing.

3. Quality Control Problems

Supplements aren't regulated like prescription drugs in Canada. Different manufacturers use different testing methods, making it hard to compare products. What's on the label may not match what's in the capsule.

4. Hidden Vitamin K2

Many nattokinase supplements contain vitamin K2, which can interfere with blood thinners like warfarin. Some manufacturers remove it, others don't, and labels don't always tell you which.

Important Safety Warning: If you take any blood-thinning medications (warfarin, apixaban, rivaroxaban, clopidogrel, ASA) or have bleeding disorders, do not take nattokinase without discussing it with your doctor first. The combination could increase bleeding risk significantly.

Who Should Avoid Nattokinase?

  • Anyone taking blood thinners or antiplatelets
  • People with bleeding disorders
  • Those scheduled for surgery (stop at least 2 weeks before)
  • Anyone with active ulcers or recent stroke
  • People taking high-dose fish oil, ginkgo, or regular NSAIDs

The Bottom Line

Honest Assessment: Nattokinase has some plausible benefits and appears relatively safe for healthy people not on other blood-affecting medications. However, the best-designed long-term study found no benefit for preventing heart disease. The supplement industry's quality control issues and absorption questions make it hard to recommend confidently.

If You're Considering Nattokinase:

Questions to Ask Yourself:

  • Am I taking any medications that affect bleeding or clotting?
  • Do I have any bleeding disorders or ulcer history?
  • Am I looking for proven heart disease prevention or just curious about "natural" options?
  • Have I discussed this with my family doctor?
  • Am I choosing a product standardized in FU (not just mg)?
  • Do I understand this isn't proven to prevent heart attacks or strokes?

Better-Proven Alternatives

If you're interested in heart health and circulation, consider these evidence-based approaches instead:

  • Regular exercise - 150 minutes per week of moderate activity
  • Mediterranean diet - Rich in fish, olive oil, vegetables
  • Blood pressure management - If elevated, work with your doctor
  • Smoking cessation - The most important step for circulation
  • Stress management - Chronic stress affects heart health
Remember: No supplement can replace proven lifestyle measures for heart health. The fundamentals - good nutrition, regular exercise, not smoking, and managing stress - remain your best tools for cardiovascular wellness.
