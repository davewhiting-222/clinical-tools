Tuberculosis (TB) Risk Screening

Prepared by your family doctor August 11, 2026

What Is Tuberculosis?

Tuberculosis (TB) is a serious infection caused by bacteria called Mycobacterium tuberculosis. It most often affects the lungs, but can involve other parts of the body. TB spreads through the air when a person with active TB coughs, sneezes, or speaks — making early identification important, especially in shared living environments like Long-Term Care Homes.

Why screening matters: Many people carry TB bacteria without feeling sick (called latent TB — TB that is "sleeping" in the body). Identifying people at higher risk early helps prevent TB from becoming active and spreading to others, particularly in vulnerable settings.

Latent TB vs. Active TB

Feature Latent TB Infection Active TB Disease
Symptoms None — person feels well Cough, fever, night sweats, weight loss
Contagious? No Yes (if lungs are affected)
Can it progress? Yes — up to 10% lifetime risk Already progressed — needs treatment now
Treatment needed? Sometimes (preventive therapy) Yes — combination antibiotic therapy
A note of reassurance: Having risk factors for TB does not mean you have TB. This questionnaire simply helps us decide whether further testing (such as a TB skin test or blood test) is appropriate for you. There is no judgment here — only good preventive care.

What Happens After This Questionnaire?

Your responses will be reviewed by a healthcare provider. Depending on the results, one or more of the following steps may be recommended:

No risk factors found: No further TB testing is required at this time. You will still receive regular health monitoring as part of your care plan.
Risk factors identified (but no symptoms): A TB skin test (also called a Tuberculin Skin Test, or TST) or a blood test called an IGRA (Interferon-Gamma Release Assay) may be arranged. These tests are simple, safe, and painless. A positive result does not automatically mean you have active TB — it just means further follow-up is needed.
Symptoms present: If you are currently experiencing symptoms that could be consistent with active TB — particularly a prolonged cough, fever, night sweats, or unexplained weight loss — please let a staff member know right away. A chest X-ray and additional testing will be arranged promptly. This is for your protection and the protection of those around you.

A Word About Treatment

If you are found to have latent TB (TB bacteria present but not causing illness), your doctor may discuss preventive treatment options with you. In Canada, this often involves taking one or two antibiotic medications for several months. This is highly effective and greatly reduces your chance of ever developing active TB.2

Active TB is treated with a combination of antibiotics, usually over 6 months or longer. TB is fully treatable when caught and managed correctly.3

Your privacy matters: All information on this form is kept strictly confidential within your care team. TB screening is a routine part of entering a Long-Term Care Home — it reflects our commitment to keeping everyone in our community as healthy as possible.

Quick Reference: Who Should Be Tested?

Risk Factor Why It Increases Risk
Born in or lived in a high-prevalence country TB rates are significantly higher in many parts of the world; people may carry latent TB for many years after leaving4
Indigenous background Ongoing social and structural inequities contribute to higher TB rates in some communities in Canada1
Weakened immune system Latent TB is more likely to become active when the immune system is suppressed5
History of substance use Associated with higher exposure risk and factors that can weaken the immune system6
Previous TB or non-resolving pneumonia May suggest prior TB exposure or untreated infection3
