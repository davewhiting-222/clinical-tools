Managing Loose Bowel Movements on a Low-Carb Diet

Prepared by your family doctor — Neurogenic Bowel Guidance for Spinal Cord Injury September 3, 2026

When you have a spinal cord injury, loose or watery stool makes bowel care unpredictable and raises your risk of skin breakdown and unplanned leakage. The goal is to get your stool back to a firm, soft-formed consistency — think of a smooth, soft log rather than something watery or liquid.

⚠ Important before you change anything: Loose stool can sometimes look like diarrhea but actually be liquid leaking around a hard blockage higher up — called a fecal impaction. Because reduced sensation may make it hard to feel a blockage forming, please work through Step 1 below before adjusting your diet or medications.

Step 2: Immediate Safety Steps

  • Do not take anti-diarrheal medicine (such as Imodium® / loperamide) unless your care team has specifically told you to — it can worsen a hidden blockage.
  • Protect your skin: Apply a barrier cream (zinc oxide, dimethicone, or petroleum jelly) after every cleanup to prevent irritation over your buttocks and tailbone.
  • Keep your scheduled bowel care routine — digital stimulation or manual evacuation at your usual time — even if stool is leaking between sessions.
  • Only pause your regular stool softener or laxative (docusate, senna, PEG/MiraLAX, magnesium) after you have confirmed Step 1 is clear. Check with your care team about when to restart them.

Step 3: Understanding Fibre — It's Not All the Same

Not all fibre affects loose stool in the same way. Knowing the difference helps you make smarter food and supplement choices.

Type of Fibre Examples Effect on Loose Stool
Rapidly fermentable fibre Inulin, chicory root fibre, FOS (fructooligosaccharides) Can cause gas and make stool looser — often hidden in "keto-friendly" bars and protein products
Bulking / gelling fibre Calcium polycarbophil, psyllium husk (controlled dose) Helps firm stool without heavy fermentation — preferred right now
Rough raw fibre Raw salads, raw broccoli or cabbage, vegetable skins, seeds, wheat bran, oat bran Can speed transit further while your gut is sensitive — cook and peel vegetables for now

Step 4: Foods to Favour Right Now

These foods are gentle on a sensitive gut, low in rapidly fermentable fibre, and compatible with a low-carbohydrate eating pattern:

  • Avocado — provides gelling soluble fibre and healthy fat without fermentable fibre
  • Well-cooked, peeled vegetables: zucchini, yellow squash, eggplant, asparagus tips, cauliflower, carrots
  • Chia seeds — pre-soaked in water, unsweetened almond milk, or broth until fully gelled (not eaten dry)
  • Cooked mushrooms — low-carb and gentle on the gut when cooked
  • Eggs — easy to digest and nutrient-dense
  • Plain grilled or baked protein: salmon, chicken, lean ground beef, canned tuna or salmon
Tip: Cooking and peeling vegetables removes most of the rough outer fibre that can speed things up. While things settle, favour soft-cooked over raw.

Step 5: Hidden Triggers to Check For

These ingredients are common in low-carb and "keto-friendly" products and are frequent causes of loose stool. Check labels carefully:

Trigger Where It Hides What to Do
Sugar alcohols: maltitol, sorbitol, xylitol, isomalt, erythritol Low-carb bars, sugar-free chocolate or candy, keto treats, protein powders Avoid products containing these for now; switch to products sweetened with stevia or monk fruit
Added fermentable fibre: inulin, chicory root fibre, FOS Keto bars, fibre supplements marketed as low-carb Read ingredient lists — avoid products listing these at the top
Large amounts of added fat or oil: MCT oil, "bulletproof" coffee, heavy cream in excess, large amounts of mayonnaise Keto coffee drinks, high-fat snacks, dressings Stick to fat that comes naturally bound in whole foods — avocado, eggs, salmon, olive oil in normal cooking amounts

Step 6: Fluids and Electrolytes

Loose stool means you're losing more fluid and salt than usual. This matters more for you than for most people, because a spinal cord injury can affect how your body manages fluid and blood pressure shifts.

  • Drink water steadily through the day rather than in large amounts at once.
  • Add a source of sodium and potassium — a cup of broth, a low-sugar electrolyte drink (such as Hydralyte® or a plain oral rehydration sachet), or salted food works well.
  • Avoid caffeine and alcohol until things have settled, as both increase fluid loss.
Why this matters: A low-carbohydrate diet also causes the kidneys to release more sodium in the first few weeks. Combined with loose stool losses, you may feel lightheaded, fatigued, or have muscle cramps — all signs that you need more salt and fluid.

Step 7: Fibre Supplements

Only start these once Step 1 is clear — do not add fibre supplements if there is any chance of a blockage.

Calcium Polycarbophil (FiberCon® or generic)

1–2 tablets (625–1250 mg — check the product you are using), taken 1–2 times daily with approximately 125–175 mL (4–6 oz) of water. This type of fibre absorbs water in the bowel and forms a gel that helps firm stool without significant fermentation.

Psyllium Husk (sugar-free / unflavoured)

Start with ½ to 1 teaspoon once daily stirred into 125–175 mL (4–6 oz) of water, increasing to twice daily if tolerated. Always check the label for added maltitol or sorbitol, which can worsen loose stool.

A note on expectations: Fibre supplements take 2–4 days to show their full effect. Start low, go slow, and give each change a few days before deciding whether it's working.

When to Contact Your Clinic

Contact the same day (or call 911 / go to Emergency) if you have:

  • Any autonomic dysreflexia symptoms — sudden severe headache, flushing, sweating above your injury level, or slow heart rate (if injury at T6 or above)
  • Fever, chills, or severe belly pain
  • Blood in the stool
  • Any new redness, skin breakdown, blistering, or open area on your buttocks, tailbone, or perianal area
  • Any uncertainty about whether this could be a blockage

Contact within 24 hours if:

  • No improvement despite the changes above
  • You are not confident this is diet-related
  • You have questions about restarting your regular bowel medications
