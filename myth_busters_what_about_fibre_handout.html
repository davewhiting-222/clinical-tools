Myth-Busters: What About Fibre?

Prepared by your family doctor
December 2024
Common Myth: "You can't get enough fibre on a low-carb diet."

The Reality: You can absolutely maintain or even increase your fibre intake while following a low-carb lifestyle. The key is understanding which foods provide fibre and how to count it properly.

Understanding Fibre: The Basics

Fibre is a type of carbohydrate that your body can't digest. Instead of being broken down for energy, it passes through your digestive system mostly intact, providing important health benefits along the way.

Why Fibre Matters:
  • Supports healthy digestion and regular bowel movements
  • Helps maintain stable blood sugar levels
  • Feeds beneficial gut bacteria
  • May help reduce cholesterol levels
  • Promotes feelings of fullness

Net Carbs vs. Total Carbs: What's the Difference?

This is where many people get confused when starting a low-carb approach:

Total Carbs

The complete amount of carbohydrates in a food, including sugars, starches, and fibre.

Net Carbs

The carbohydrates that actually affect your blood sugar. Calculated as: Total Carbs - Fibre = Net Carbs

Canadian Example: A medium avocado contains about 17g total carbs, but 13g of that is fibre. So the net carbs are only 4g (17 - 13 = 4). This means the avocado has minimal impact on your blood sugar while providing excellent nutrition and fibre.

High-Fibre, Low-Carb Vegetables

Many vegetables are naturally high in fibre while being low in net carbs. Here are some excellent options available in Canadian grocery stores:

Vegetable (1 cup) Total Carbs Fibre Net Carbs
Broccoli (chopped) 6g 2g 4g
Brussels sprouts 8g 3g 5g
Cauliflower (chopped) 5g 2g 3g
Spinach (raw) 1g 1g 0g
Kale (chopped) 7g 1g 6g
Cabbage (chopped) 6g 2g 4g

Why Fibre Intake Can Actually Improve on Low-Carb

The Replacement Effect: When you reduce refined grains, sugars, and processed foods, you naturally make room for more nutrient-dense, fibre-rich vegetables. Many people discover they're eating more vegetables than ever before!

Common Scenarios Where Fibre Increases

  1. Vegetable Focus: Instead of filling up on bread and pasta, meals center around large portions of fibrous vegetables
  2. Quality Over Quantity: Choosing nutrient-dense foods means getting more fibre per calorie
  3. Mindful Eating: Paying attention to food choices often leads to better vegetable intake
  4. Cooking at Home: Preparing more meals from scratch typically increases vegetable consumption

Additional Low-Carb Fibre Sources

Beyond Vegetables:
  • Nuts and Seeds: Almonds, chia seeds, flaxseeds, hemp hearts
  • Low-Sugar Fruits: Berries, avocados
  • Coconut Products: Unsweetened coconut flakes
  • Olives: Green or black varieties

Monitoring Your Progress

Pay Attention to Your Body: Good fibre intake should support regular, comfortable bowel movements. If you experience digestive changes when adjusting your eating pattern, this may be normal as your system adapts, but discuss any concerns with your healthcare provider.

Signs of Adequate Fibre Intake

  • Regular bowel movements (typically daily)
  • Feeling satisfied after meals
  • Stable energy levels
  • No significant digestive discomfort
Remember: The goal isn't to maximize fibre at all costs, but to maintain adequate intake while supporting your overall health goals. Many people find they feel better and have improved digestion when focusing on nutrient-dense, naturally fibrous foods rather than processed "high-fibre" products.
