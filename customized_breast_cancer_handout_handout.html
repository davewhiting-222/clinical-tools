Understanding Your Breast Cancer Diagnosis

Prepared by your family doctor

First, Take a Deep Breath

I know receiving a cancer diagnosis can feel overwhelming. You're not alone in this journey. Let's walk through your specific results together and discuss what this means for your care moving forward. Your cancer has several favorable characteristics that put you in a good position for treatment and recovery.

Understanding Your Pathology Results

The Good News About Your Cancer

Your cancer is small and low-grade:

  • Size: Only 6 millimeters across (about the size of a pencil eraser)
  • Type: Tubular carcinoma - a favorable subtype that typically grows slowly
  • Grade: Grade 1 (low grade) - the cancer cells look very similar to normal cells
  • DCIS component: A small area of pre-cancer cells, also low grade
  • No lymphovascular invasion: The cancer hasn't spread to nearby blood or lymph vessels

Hormone Receptor Status

Your cancer tested positive for hormone receptors, which is actually good news:

Receptor Your Results What This Means
Estrogen Receptor (ER) 91-100% positive Hormone therapy will be highly effective
Progesterone Receptor (PgR) 11-20% positive Additional hormone sensitivity
HER2 Equivocal (2+) Need additional FISH test to confirm

Why hormone-positive is good news: About 90-100% of your cancer cells respond to estrogen. This means hormone-targeted treatments will be highly effective at preventing recurrence. You're in the group of patients who benefit most from endocrine (hormone) therapy.

Your Next Steps Timeline

This Week: HER2 FISH Test

We need one more test (FISH) to determine if HER2-targeted drugs are necessary. This is routine and results should be ready within a week.

Next 1-2 Weeks: Surgical Consultation

You'll meet with a breast surgeon to plan a small lumpectomy (breast-conserving surgery) and sentinel node biopsy to check if cancer has spread to nearby lymph nodes.

After Surgery: Treatment Planning

Based on final pathology results, we'll finalize your treatment plan, which will likely include radiation therapy and hormone therapy.

Expected Treatment Plan

Surgery

A lumpectomy will remove the cancer while preserving most of your breast. The sentinel node biopsy will check the first lymph nodes that cancer would spread to, helping us stage your cancer accurately.

Radiation Therapy

A short course of radiation therapy after surgery will significantly reduce the risk of cancer coming back in the same breast. This is typically given 5 days a week for 3-5 weeks.

Hormone Therapy

Because your cancer is hormone-positive, you'll take medication to block estrogen for 5-10 years. Options include:

  • Tamoxifen: Blocks estrogen receptors
  • Aromatase inhibitors: Reduce estrogen production (for postmenopausal women)

We'll discuss which option is best for you based on your age and menopausal status.

What About Chemotherapy?

Good news: Because your tumor is small, low-grade, and hormone-positive, chemotherapy is unlikely to be recommended. Your excellent prognosis means the benefits of chemotherapy would be minimal while still carrying side effects.

When to Contact Our Office

Call us immediately if you notice:

  • New lumps in either breast or underarm area
  • Unusual breast pain that persists
  • Changes in breast shape, size, or skin appearance
  • Nipple discharge (especially if bloody)
  • Skin dimpling, puckering, or redness
  • Persistent bone pain or shortness of breath

Your cancer has many favorable characteristics - it's small, low-grade, and hormone-positive. While any cancer diagnosis is concerning, you have an excellent prognosis with appropriate treatment.
