Understanding the Real Problem: It's Not Your Pancreas

Since your C-peptide is normal, your pancreas is doing its job well. The issue is that your body needs large amounts of insulin—both your own and the injected doses—to achieve good blood sugar control. This points to insulin resistance rather than insulin deficiency.

The "Cellular Lock and Key" Problem

Think of insulin as a key that unlocks your muscle and liver cells to let glucose in. In insulin resistance, fat deposits inside these cells act like rust in the lock, making it much harder for the key to work. Your body compensates by making (and requiring) massive amounts of insulin to force the doors open.

The Hidden Role of Insulin: More Than Blood Sugar Control

While we often focus on insulin's role in managing blood sugar, its primary function is actually as a storage hormone. When insulin levels remain high—even when your glucose looks good—it:

  • Forces your body to store energy as fat
  • Blocks your ability to burn stored fat for fuel
  • Creates a cycle where the fat causing resistance can't be cleared

Shifting Your Perspective

Our goal isn't just perfect blood sugar numbers—it's achieving those numbers with the least amount of insulin possible. This is what unlocks your body's ability to use its own stored energy efficiently and potentially eliminate the need for injected insulin.

Strategy 2: Leverage Natural Metabolic Activators

When insulin pathways are resistant, we can use compounds that work through alternative mechanisms. Berberine is a plant-derived compound that activates your cellular "master switch" (AMPK), helping muscles take up glucose independently of insulin.

Important: Discuss berberine supplementation with me before starting, as it can enhance insulin sensitivity and may require dose adjustments to prevent low blood sugars.

Understanding Your Lab Results During Transition

As you become more insulin sensitive and potentially wean off injected insulin, your lab results may show some counterintuitive patterns. Here's how to interpret them:

The Key Test: Fasting Insulin Level

This is the most important marker to distinguish between true resistance and healthy fat adaptation:

  • High fasting insulin + High glucose: True resistance persists
  • Low fasting insulin + Mildly elevated glucose (5.8-6.5 mmol/L): Healthy fat-adapted state where muscles preferentially burn fat to spare glucose for your brain
