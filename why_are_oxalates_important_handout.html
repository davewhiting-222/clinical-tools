Understanding Oxalates: What You Need to Know

Prepared by your family doctor
December 2024
Why This Matters: Understanding oxalates helps you make informed dietary choices and reduce your risk of kidney stones and other health issues. With the right knowledge, you can enjoy a balanced diet while protecting your health.

What Are Oxalates?

Oxalates (also called oxalic acid) are natural compounds found in many plants. Your body also produces oxalates as a waste product during normal metabolism. In small amounts, oxalates are harmless. However, problems can arise when oxalate levels become too high in your body.1

When oxalates combine with calcium in your urine, they can form crystals. These crystals may grow into kidney stones - hard deposits that can cause severe pain when they pass through your urinary system.2

Foods High in Oxalates

Many healthy foods contain oxalates, which is why complete avoidance isn't usually recommended. Instead, understanding which foods are highest in oxalates helps you make balanced choices.

Very High Oxalate Foods (>100mg per serving)

Food Serving Size Oxalate Content
Spinach (cooked) 1 cup 755 mg
Rhubarb 1 cup 541 mg
Almonds 1 ounce (24 nuts) 122 mg
Beets 1 cup 152 mg
Dark chocolate 1 ounce 120 mg

Moderate Oxalate Foods (10-100mg per serving)

  • Sweet potatoes
  • Black tea
  • Raspberries
  • Carrots
  • Celery
  • Green beans
  • Peanuts

Medical Conditions Associated with Oxalates

Kidney Stones

About 80% of kidney stones are calcium oxalate stones. These form when there's too much oxalate and not enough calcium in the urine to bind with it.3 Risk factors include:

  • Dehydration
  • High oxalate diet
  • Low calcium intake
  • Digestive disorders affecting calcium absorption
  • Family history of kidney stones

Hyperoxaluria

This condition occurs when your body produces or absorbs too much oxalate. There are three types:

  1. Primary hyperoxaluria: A rare genetic disorder where the liver produces too much oxalate
  2. Enteric hyperoxaluria: Often occurs after bowel surgery or with inflammatory bowel disease
  3. Dietary hyperoxaluria: Results from eating too many high-oxalate foods
Watch for These Symptoms: Severe back or side pain, blood in urine, frequent urination, nausea, or vomiting may indicate kidney stones. Contact your healthcare provider immediately if you experience these symptoms.

What to Do When Oxalates Are Elevated

Dietary Modifications

The Good News: You don't need to eliminate all high-oxalate foods. Smart strategies can help you reduce oxalate absorption while maintaining a nutritious diet.

Key Strategies:

  1. Pair oxalate-rich foods with calcium: Eat calcium-rich foods (dairy, fortified plant milks, leafy greens) with high-oxalate meals. Calcium binds to oxalates in your intestines, reducing absorption.4
  2. Stay well hydrated: Drink at least 2-3 litres of water daily to dilute oxalates in your urine.
  3. Limit vitamin C supplements: High doses (>1000mg daily) can increase oxalate production. Food sources of vitamin C are fine.5
  4. Cook high-oxalate vegetables: Boiling reduces oxalate content by 30-90%. Discard the cooking water.
  5. Moderate sodium intake: High sodium increases calcium loss in urine, making stones more likely to form.

Medical Management

Your doctor may recommend:

  • 24-hour urine collection: To measure oxalate, calcium, and other substances
  • Potassium citrate: Medication that makes urine less acidic and reduces stone formation
  • Probiotics: Specific strains may help break down oxalates in the gut
  • Regular monitoring: Blood tests and imaging to track kidney function

Practical Action Plan

Your Oxalate Management Checklist:

Track your daily water intake - aim for 8-10 glasses

Include calcium-rich foods with meals containing high-oxalate foods

Cook and drain high-oxalate vegetables rather than eating them raw

Limit spinach, rhubarb, and beets if you've had kidney stones

Choose low-oxalate alternatives: romaine lettuce instead of spinach, cauliflower instead of beets

Keep a food diary if your doctor recommends monitoring oxalate intake

Follow up with blood and urine tests as recommended

Sample Low-Oxalate Meal Ideas

  • Breakfast: Greek yogurt with blueberries and a sprinkle of flax seeds
  • Lunch: Chicken salad with romaine lettuce, cucumbers, and avocado
  • Dinner: Grilled salmon with steamed broccoli and white rice
  • Snacks: Apple slices with cheese, or a small handful of sunflower seeds
Remember: The goal isn't to avoid all oxalates, but to manage them wisely. Many high-oxalate foods are nutritious and can be part of a healthy diet when balanced properly with calcium and adequate hydration.
