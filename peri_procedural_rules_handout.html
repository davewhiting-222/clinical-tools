Peri-Procedural Rules for Low-Carb/Keto Diabetic Patients

Prepared by your family doctor
December 28, 2024
Why Special Rules Are Necessary: If you have diabetes and follow a low-carbohydrate or ketogenic diet, your medication needs are already significantly reduced. When you must fast for a medical procedure (surgery, colonoscopy, etc.), the combination of fasting, dehydration, and your current medications can cause severe metabolic emergencies if not managed correctly.
Important Note: The majority of patients following a low-carbohydrate or ketogenic diet will have already stopped most of these medications due to improved glucose control. Nevertheless, it is best to go through the safety check to ensure optimal peri-procedural management.
Important: Discuss this handout with your prescribing physician and surgical team well in advance of your procedure. Do not make medication changes without medical supervision.

The "SSTOP" Protocol for SGLT2 Inhibitors

SGLT2 inhibitor medications include:

  • Empagliflozin (Jardiance)
  • Dapagliflozin (Forxiga)
  • Canagliflozin (Invokana)
  • Ertugliflozin (Steglatro)

These medications carry a risk of triggering Euglycemic Diabetic Ketoacidosis (euDKA) - a dangerous condition where acid builds up in your blood. Fasting and dehydration are primary triggers.1

Critical Action Required: You must stop your SGLT2 inhibitor a minimum of 3 to 4 days prior to any scheduled surgery, colonoscopy, or period of enforced fasting.
  • Canagliflozin, Dapagliflozin, Empagliflozin: Stop 3 days prior
  • Ertugliflozin: May require 4 days to clear your system

Managing "Hypo-Makers" (Insulin Secretagogues)

Insulin secretagogues are medications that force your pancreas to secrete insulin regardless of your blood sugar level:

  • Sulfonylureas (insulin secretagogues): Gliclazide (Diamicron), Glyburide, Glimepiride
  • Meglitinides (insulin secretagogues): Repaglinide, Nateglinide
Action Required: Discontinue these insulin secretagogue medications while you are fasting for your procedure. Taking them while fasting will drive your blood sugar down to dangerously low levels that could be life-threatening.

Insulin Dose Adjustments

Because you are fasting and not consuming carbohydrates, your insulin requirements will drop drastically.2

Short-Acting/Mealtime
(Humalog, NovoRapid, Apidra)		 STOP completely No carbohydrates to cover

(30/70, 25/75 mixes)		 STOP completely Contains short-acting component

Timeline for Medication Changes

3-4 Days Before Procedure

Stop SGLT2 inhibitors (Jardiance, Forxiga, Invokana, Steglatro)

Day Before Procedure

Confirm medication plan with your healthcare team

Day of Fasting

Stop insulin secretagogues (medications that force insulin release) and mealtime insulin
Reduce long-acting insulin as directed

After Procedure

Resume normal medications once eating normally again

Warning Signs of Euglycemic Diabetic Ketoacidosis (euDKA)

Critical Warning: With SGLT2 inhibitors, your blood sugar reading may appear completely normal (under 11-14 mmol/L or 200-250 mg/dL) even while you are in a state of severe metabolic emergency.3

Seek immediate emergency medical care if you experience:

  • Nausea or vomiting
  • Profound fatigue or weakness
  • Abdominal pain
  • Rapid, deep breathing (Kussmaul breathing)
  • Fruity breath odour
  • Confusion or difficulty concentrating

Pre-Procedure Checklist

Discussed this handout with prescribing physician
Shared handout with surgical/procedural team
SGLT2 inhibitor stopped 3-4 days before procedure
Plan in place for insulin secretagogues (sulfonylureas/meglitinides)
Insulin dose adjustments confirmed with healthcare team
Emergency contact numbers readily available
Blood glucose meter and ketone testing strips available

Instructions for Your Medical Team

"This patient follows a therapeutic carbohydrate restriction protocol for diabetes management. Please be aware that their insulin and insulin secretagogue (medications that force insulin release) requirements are substantially lower than standard diabetic patients. Furthermore, if the patient has recently taken an SGLT2 inhibitor, capillary blood ketone testing (beta-hydroxybutyrate) should be prioritized over urine ketone strips or standard blood glucose alone to rule out euDKA during the peri-procedural fasting period."

Remember: These precautions exist because your low-carb/keto lifestyle has already improved your metabolic health significantly. With proper planning, you can safely undergo medical procedures while maintaining these benefits.
